# Mood board — light, all the way real

The manifesto asks for either true abstraction or full realism. Since the critique, everything has been abstraction or "its own material". Realism was only ever attempted once (*West Window*), in the cheap middle. Light is the right subject for it, because light is what a screen literally is.

- **Dan Flavin**, corner pieces and barriers (1963–96). Coloured fluorescent tubes (pink, yellow, green, blue, red, daylight, cool white, ultraviolet); the work is not the tube but the wall washed with mixed colour, light bounced off architecture. *Take:* coloured light and a white room; the colour you see is the mix on the walls, which nobody paints.
- **James Turrell**, *Wedgework*s. Planes of coloured light that read as solid walls in a dark room; the space itself becomes uncertain. *Take:* saturated light as a volume; darkness.
- **Hiroshi Sugimoto**, *Theaters* (1976–). One exposure lasting a whole film: the screen burns white from everything projected on it and lights the empty cinema. *Take:* an image made by accumulating light over time.
- **Anthony McCall**, *Solid Light* works (1973–). A projected line becomes a cone of light in haze. *Take:* line becomes volume; light in time.
- **Robert Irwin**, *Scrim veil—Black rectangle—Natural light* (1977). A translucent fabric across a room in daylight; you perceive the space more than any object. *Take:* almost nothing, exactly lit.

## For me

A path tracer builds a picture exactly like Sugimoto's cinema: light accumulating sample by sample, from black sparks to a developed image. So: **a white room lit only by coloured tubes, rendered by real light transport (bounces, colour bleeding, soft shadows), developing live in the viewer's browser.** The physics is real. The colour mixing in corners and on walls is emergent. The time is the time an exposure takes.

What could go wrong: Flavin pastiche; the "3D render" look (the cheap middle again). It has to look like light, not like CG. Judge against a photograph of a Flavin, not against other renders.
