# Mood board — before the fifth work

What I'm taking from each, not what they look like.

- **Gerhard Richter, squeegee abstracts** (from the late 1970s on). He sets the conditions (colours, blade, pressure, direction) and the paint does the rest. "I want to end up with a picture that I haven't planned." The surface is built *and destroyed* by the same drag, and the layers underneath show through where the blade skipped. [Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artists/gerhard-richter-1841/how-did-richter-make-his-paintings) · [Gagosian interview](https://gagosian.com/quarterly/2021/02/22/interview-gerhard-richter/)
- **Richter, *Strip* (2011).** A scan of a squeegee painting, halved and mirrored thousands of times and stretched into lines: a digital operation applied to a physical accident. *Don't copy the strips. Take the permission: the computer may be the squeegee.*
- **Hiroshi Sugimoto, *Lightning Fields*.** No camera; he discharged electricity straight onto film. The image doesn't depict a process, it *is* one. That's "all the way" without illustration.
- **Thomas Ruff, *jpegs*.** Compression blocks blown up to museum scale; the medium's own artifact becomes the subject.
- **Steina & Woody Vasulka.** Video feedback and the scan processor: the signal folded back on itself. [Fondation Langlois](https://www.fondation-langlois.org/html/e/page.php?NumPage=456)
- **Rosa Menkman.** Artifacts are "the failure of media to disappear". A warning too: glitch is now a genre, a look you can buy as a filter. [Glitch Moment(um)](https://networkcultures.org/blog/publication/no-04-the-glitch-momentum-rosa-menkman/)
- **Tauba Auerbach, *Fold* paintings.** The canvas is creased, then spray-painted under raking light, then flattened. The image is the record of a state the object is no longer in. [Art in America](https://www.artnews.com/art-in-america/aia-reviews/tauba-auerbach-2-61291/)
- **Agnes Martin.** Hand-ruled lines that *almost* hold. The tremor is the content.
- **Bridget Riley, *Cataract 3* (1967).** Nothing but modulated lines, and the eye does the rest.

## The thread

In every one of these, the artist builds a *procedure* and accepts what comes out of it; none of them draws a picture of a thing. The accident is in the material: paint, film, a signal, a file.

## For me

My material is a buffer of numbers that can be dragged, fed back, resampled and quantised. So: **a digital squeegee.** A blade drags across a float pixel buffer, picking colour up, laying it down, skipping, chattering. Same blade, same buffer, again and again, over layers it laid itself. No simulated paint, no sheen or impasto: the pixels *are* the paint. I set the conditions and don't know the outcome.

What could go wrong: it becomes a Richter pastiche. The exploration has to lead somewhere he couldn't go (pickup rules no physical paint obeys, blend modes, precision loss, blades a thousand times finer than a pixel or wider than the canvas), or it's not done.
