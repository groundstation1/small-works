# Mood board — a body, moving

Taking stock: grey texture (*Drag*, *Room*, *Self-Portrait II*, *Grey*), flat colour in motion (*Found*), line and density (*Pour*), light (*Exposure*). Nothing with a body: movement, weight, falling.

- **Étienne-Jules Marey**, geometric chronophotographs (1883–). A runner in a black suit with white strips along the limbs, photographed many times a second on one plate. The body disappears; the geometry of movement remains as fans and combs of white lines. *Take:* the method itself. Limbs as lines, time as repetition on one plate.
- **Eadweard Muybridge**, *The Human Figure in Motion* (1887). Grids of sequential frames. *Take:* the sequence as a grid, another way to hang a series.
- **Marcel Duchamp**, *Nude Descending a Staircase, No. 2* (1912). A figure multiplied into a cascade of planes. *Take:* motion as accumulation. *Avoid:* stairs; a simulated figure falling down stairs is a joke about Duchamp, an illustration.
- **Trisha Brown**, drawings made by dancing on paper (2000s). Charcoal in the hands and feet; the drawing is the trace of the whole body's sweeps. *Take:* the body as the drawing tool, not the subject.
- **William Forsythe**, *Improvisation Technologies* (1999). A dancer draws imaginary lines in space; geometry generated by a body. *Take:* movement as line.

## For me

A physically simulated jointed body (bones, joints, gravity, ground friction), driven only by simple rhythmic muscles: joint angles swinging at their own rates. It is not a person and I don't animate it. Given random rhythms it may walk, stagger, crawl, flail or fall; I can't predict which. Recorded the way Marey did: only the limbs, as white lines on black, sampled at a fixed interval on one wide plate.

What could go wrong: a stick figure (clip art); a "physics ragdoll" demo; comedy. It has to look like Marey's plates, where the figure is gone and only the geometry of the movement is left.
