# Mood board — line and density

After nine works of fields (grey textures, flat shapes, colour areas), almost all minimal, there's no *line* in the gallery and nothing *dense*. This board is the other end: many marks, layered energy, drawing.

- **Julie Mehretu.** Architectural drawings, precise and cold, buried under swarms of gestural marks and shards of colour; depth from translucent layers stacked and sanded. *Take:* layering precise and wild marks; density with depth, not just clutter.
- **Cy Twombly, blackboard paintings (1966–71).** Looping chalk lines on grey, handwriting exercises with the writing removed. Pure rhythm and repetition, never quite the same loop twice. *Take:* the loop as the unit; rows; the tremor of repetition.
- **Brice Marden, *Cold Mountain* (1988–91).** Lines grown from the columns of Hanshan's poems: the structure of text becomes a lattice of interlaced calligraphic loops. *Take:* a hidden structure (columns) under free line; interlacing.
- **Gego, *Reticulárea* (1969–).** Nets of wire hung in space, triangulated but never a grid; the shadows are part of the drawing. *Take:* line as a net and not a contour; irregular structure.
- **Henri Michaux, mescaline drawings (1955–).** Dense, shivering fields and columns of tiny marks; the line trembles because the hand is overwhelmed. *Take:* density as a state; vibration.
- **Sol LeWitt, wall drawings.** An instruction, executed by others; the same rule draws a different wall each time. *Take:* the procedure as the work.
- **Jackson Pollock, *Autumn Rhythm* (1950).** All-over, no centre; skeins in layers of black, white, brown. The line is made by physics, a thread of paint falling and coiling, not by drawing. *Take:* this is the key.
- **Hanne Darboven.** Rooms of framed sheets of written counting; the grid of sheets is the work. *Take:* a series can be a wall.

## The science

- Herczyński, Cernuschi & Mahadevan, *Painting with drops, jets, and sheets* (Physics Today, 2011): Pollock's skeins come from the **coiling instability** of a falling viscous jet, superposed on the movement of his hand. [Physics Today](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/painting-with-drops-jets-and-sheets)
- The **fluid-mechanical sewing machine**: a viscous thread falling onto a moving belt draws coils, alternating loops, W patterns, meanders and straight lines depending on belt speed. [Chiu-Webster & Lister](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/231908986_The_fall_of_a_viscous_thread_onto_a_moving_surface_A_'fluid-mechanical_sewing_machine') · [Brun et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.1378)
- The **geometric model** (Brun, Audoly, Ribe, Eaves, Lister, *Liquid ropes*, PRL 2015; revisited in [arXiv:2410.21503](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21503v1)): three ODEs in arclength for the contact point (r, ψ) and the laid tangent θ, with a fitted curvature law. V = belt speed / coiling speed.

## For me

The line should come from real physics, the way Pollock's did: **a gesture (the hand's path and speed) fed into the geometric model, and the thread draws what it draws.** Slow hand, coils; faster, loops and meanders; fast, a straight line. Layer many pours for density (Pollock, Mehretu), perhaps along a hidden structure (Marden's columns, Twombly's rows).

What could go wrong: it becomes a Pollock screensaver, or a physics-demo plot. It has to be judged as drawing, next to Twombly and Marden, not as a simulation.
